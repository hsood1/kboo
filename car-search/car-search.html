Leasing 101
Who Pays for What?
Leasing = paying to use something you don't own. Here's exactly how it works.
Your Responsibility
💸 You pay for this
💳
Monthly Payments
Every month, on time. Missing payments damages credit and can trigger repossession — same stakes as an apartment.
🛡️
Full Coverage Insurance (Required)
The leasing company mandates comprehensive + collision — minimum liability is not allowed. Must be active before you drive off the lot.
🔧
Routine Maintenance
Oil changes, tire rotations, wiper blades — yours. Toyota and Hyundai cover first 2 years / 25K miles free on new cars; after that it's on you.
⛽
Gas or Charging
Always yours. Hybrids save ~$40–60/mo vs gas-only in San Diego. Tesla EVs save even more if she has home charging (~$30/mo).
🛣️
Mileage Overages — Watch This
Standard = 10,000–12,000 mi/yr. Every mile over = $0.15–0.25 at lease end. At 15K mi/yr on a 10K lease = ~$1,500 extra/yr. Estimate real driving before signing.
🗑️
Disposition Fee ($350–400) at Return
Charged when you hand the car back. Avoidable if you immediately lease or buy again from the same brand.
Covered For You
✅ Manufacturer & dealer pays
🔩
Mechanical Breakdowns (Warranty)
Engine, transmission, hybrid battery failures = warranty-covered. Toyota: 3yr/36K basic + 10yr/150K hybrid. Hyundai: 5yr/60K + 10yr/100K powertrain.
🔁
Recall Repairs
Safety recalls are 100% free — bring it in, they fix it, no cost, no hassle.
📱
Software & Tech Glitches
Infotainment bugs, sensor malfunctions — all warranty-covered. Just schedule a service appointment.
🏠
No Depreciation Stress
At lease end, just return the keys. No selling it, no trade-in negotiation, no being stuck with an aging car.
🚨
Roadside Assistance (Included)
Toyota and Hyundai include manufacturer roadside — flat tires, lockouts, towing. Separate from your insurance.

Shopping Strategy
Tips for September
How to get the best deal when she's ready to shop in ~3 months.
01
Time it right — end of quarter is king
September 30 = end of Q3. Salespeople have monthly and quarterly quotas. The last few days of September, dealers are more willing to deal than almost any other time of year. Go then.
02
Go inland for better prices
Toyota Temecula, Toyota of El Cajon, Hyundai Temecula, National City Hyundai. A 30-min drive can save $1,500–3,000 on the same car vs coastal SD stores.
03
Verify cooled seats on the specific car
Always confirm ventilated seats are present on the actual unit — not just the trim level in general. Check the window sticker and feel for perforation holes in the seat fabric.
04
Get insurance quotes before signing
Run Geico, Mercury, and Progressive quotes before the dealer appointment. Insurance must be active on delivery day for leased/financed cars.
05
RAV4: Use Costco Auto or TrueCar
Toyota doesn't discount off MSRP easily, but Costco Auto Program and TrueCar pre-negotiate pricing. Often saves $500–1,500 with zero haggling.
06
Leasing: negotiate the cap cost, not the payment
Every dollar off the capitalized cost lowers your monthly payment. Don't let dealers make the monthly number the only focus. Negotiate the car price first, then apply lease terms.
07
Think honestly about mileage
San Diego is a driving city. 20 mi each way × 250 work days = 10,000 miles just commuting, before any weekend driving. Consider upgrading to a 12K mi/yr lease if that sounds tight.
08
Buy in CA — anywhere SoCal is fine
She must register in California. Temecula, El Cajon, Chula Vista, National City, Mission Valley, Kearny Mesa — all fair game. Buying in-state handles DMV registration automatically.
